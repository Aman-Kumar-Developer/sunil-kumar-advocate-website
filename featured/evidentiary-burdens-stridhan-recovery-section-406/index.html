Evidentiary Burdens in Stridhan Recovery and Section 406 Claims

Practical application of the Pratibha Rani doctrine distinguishing personal Stridhan property from joint matrimonial possessions in criminal breach of trust claims.

This page summarizes the legal framework and judicial precedents for educational and informational purposes only. It does not state or imply that Advocate Sunil Kumar Upadhyay represented a party or was involved in the matter.

The Pratibha Rani Landmark Doctrine

In the historic judgment Pratibha Rani v. Suraj Kumar (1985) 2 SCC 370, the Supreme Court settled the legal status of Stridhan. The Court ruled that Stridhan is the absolute and exclusive property of a married woman, gifted to her prior to, at the time of, or after marriage.

The husband and in-laws hold Stridhan purely in a fiduciary capacity as trustees. If the woman demands the return of her Stridhan and the husband or in-laws fail or refuse to return it, they commit the criminal offence of Criminal Breach of Trust under Section 406 IPC (Section 316 BNS).

Evidentiary Burdens and Documenting Stridhan Articles

To sustain a claim under Section 406 or Section 19(8) of the DV Act, the complainant must establish: (i) entrustment of specific articles to the accused, and (ii) a clear, recorded demand for return followed by dishonest misappropriation.

In practice, claims are substantiated through contemporaneous wedding gift lists signed by both families, purchase invoices, jeweller valuation certificates, photographs of ceremonies, and formal legal demand notices.

Defenses Against Exaggerated Stridhan Claims

Criminal courts scrutinize inflated Stridhan claims where complainants list exorbitant jewellery or assets without matching family income tax capacity. The Supreme Court requires clear proof of initial possession and entrustment before initiating criminal process for breach of trust.
