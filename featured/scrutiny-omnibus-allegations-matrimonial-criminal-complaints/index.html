Scrutiny of Omnibus Allegations in Matrimonial Criminal Complaints

Judicial thresholds applied under Section 482 CrPC / Section 528 BNSS by Delhi High Court to quash vague criminal allegations against distant matrimonial relatives.

This page summarizes the legal framework and judicial precedents for educational and informational purposes only. It does not state or imply that Advocate Sunil Kumar represented a party or was involved in the matter.

The Problem of Omnibus Averments in Matrimonial Disputes

Criminal complaints arising from matrimonial breakdown (Section 498A IPC / Section 85-86 BNS and Sections 3/4 Dowry Prohibition Act) frequently implicate not only the husband but distant in-laws, married sisters-in-law, and elderly relatives residing in separate cities.

The tendency to make general, vague, and omnibus allegations against the entire extended family without attributing specific dates, overt acts, or physical cruelty has been repeatedly deprecated by constitutional courts.

The Supreme Court Doctrines in Geeta Mehrotra & Kahkashan Kausar

In Geeta Mehrotra v. State of U.P. (2012) 10 SCC 741 and Kahkashan Kausar @ Sonam v. State of Bihar (2022) 6 SCC 599, the Supreme Court laid down that distant relatives cannot be made to face criminal trial on the basis of general allegations of dowry harassment.

Unless the FIR and police report contain specific, distinct averments disclosing overt acts of cruelty committed by each relative, prosecuting distant family members constitutes an abuse of the judicial process.

Quashing Standards under Section 482 CrPC / Section 528 BNSS

The Delhi High Court exercises inherent powers under Section 482 CrPC (Section 528 BNSS) to quash complaints and chargesheets where allegations against relatives are omnibus and unsubstantiated.

Where records establish that relatives resided separately or that the dispute was primarily a personal matrimonial conflict between the spouses, High Courts consistently terminate criminal proceedings to prevent harassment of extended family.

Key Precedents & Authorities

  • Geeta Mehrotra v. State of U.P. (2012) 10 SCC 741 — Indian Kanoon
  • Kahkashan Kausar v. State of Bihar (2022) 6 SCC 599 — Indian Kanoon
